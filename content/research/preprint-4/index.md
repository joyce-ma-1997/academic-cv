abstract:
  How does immigration policy shape firms’ decisions to organize innovation activities? This project examines the annual cap on H-1B visas and asks whether constraints on access to highly skilled foreign talent lead federal contractors to outsource innovative activities through subcontracting. We construct a novel dataset linking federal contracts and subcontracts to firm-level H-1B petitions, visa lottery outcomes, and patenting activity. The project investigates which activities firms contract out when skilled talent becomes scarce, whether recipient firms rely more heavily on highly skilled workers, and how immigration policy ultimately affects firm boundaries and innovation performance.
